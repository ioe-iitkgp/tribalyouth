Future-Ready Tribal Youth

AI, grounded technology and sustainable creative livelihoods

A field-linked learning and innovation initiative of the Centre of Excellence for Unified AI-Enabled Craft Ecosystem Platform, IIT Kharagpur. Students from remote and tribal communities explore how AI, IoT, local knowledge, traditional crafts, entrepreneurship and ecology can solve real problems in their own communities.

    01 About the programme

    Technology that
    starts from home.

    The Future-Ready Tribal Youth programme connects school students from remote and tribal regions with technology, innovation and community-based problem solving.

    Rather than treating AI, IoT and digital technologies as isolated classroom subjects, the programme encourages students to observe their own surroundings, understand local challenges and identify opportunities where technology can create meaningful social, livelihood or environmental impact.

    Students work in teams under the mentorship of IIT Kharagpur research scholars and faculty members, and develop small community-grounded projects based on the realities of their own locations.

    The programme combines

    • 01Artificial Intelligence and digital tools
    • 02Internet of Things and sensing
    • 03Low-power communication technologies
    • 04Local and indigenous knowledge
    • 05Traditional crafts and creative livelihoods
    • 06Entrepreneurship and livelihood awareness
    • 07Environmental sustainability
    • 08Community problem solving
    • 09Field observation and project development

    Our motivation

    Two worlds that were never meant to be apart.

    Many young students living in remote and tribal areas have limited opportunities for sustained exposure to emerging technologies. At the same time, their communities hold rich knowledge of traditional crafts, local materials, indigenous practices, community livelihoods, ecological systems and culturally rooted problem-solving.

    The programme seeks to connect these two worlds. Technology does not need to be separate from community, culture or livelihood.

    04 How the programme works

    From a school corridor
    to a field project.

    Community & student identification

    IIT Kharagpur teams will visit the participating schools and communities to understand:

    • local student interests
    • community challenges
    • local crafts and livelihoods
    • connectivity conditions
    • available infrastructure
    • potential field projects
    • opportunities for technology intervention

    Students are selected primarily on

    • curiosity
    • willingness to learn
    • creativity
    • teamwork
    • interest in community problems
    • motivation to participate

    Prior programming, AI or electronics knowledge is not required.

    Workshop 1: orientation & project initiation

    Venue · IIT KharagpurDate · Coming SoonOne day

    The first one-day workshop introduces students to:

    • AI and practical digital tools
    • IoT and sensing
    • communication technologies
    • creative problem solving
    • local crafts and livelihoods
    • entrepreneurship
    • ecology and sustainability
    • project development

    Each team identifies a community-grounded problem or opportunity and develops an initial project plan.

    Mentored field project

    After the first workshop, students work within their own communities. Each team is supported by:

    • an IIT Kharagpur research-scholar mentor
    • faculty supervision
    • their nominated school teacher
    • local community members where required

    Projects may involve

    • digital craft documentation
    • community mapping
    • artisan storytelling
    • local-resource monitoring
    • IoT-based sensing
    • digital catalogues
    • craft-quality assessment
    • environmental monitoring
    • low-cost technology solutions
    • market and livelihood studies
    • communication systems
    • other locally relevant innovations

    07 Community communication initiative

    A voice for the village
    with no signal.

    The programme intends to identify one remote village where conventional cellular connectivity such as Airtel, Jio, Vi or BSNL is unavailable or not practically accessible to local residents.

    Subject to technical feasibility, IIT Kharagpur will explore deployment of a Solar-Powered LoRa / LPWAN Community Communication Network.

    Mobile network: unavailable

    Swipe sideways to see the whole valley →

    Press Send a message to see how a short message hops across the valley.

    The system may consist of

    • solar-powered communication nodes
    • long-range LoRa communication
    • relay nodes
    • local communication devices
    • low-bandwidth emergency / community messaging capability

    The goal is to explore whether a low-cost communication infrastructure can provide basic communication support where conventional mobile connectivity does not reach.

    Who does what

    The communication hardware will be supported through IIT Kharagpur / project resources. Local schools, NGOs and community organisations may assist with:

    • suitable installation locations
    • local permissions
    • deployment
    • field testing
    • supervision of the installed nodes

    Subject to successful deployment and applicable institutional procedures, the hardware is intended to remain within the community after completion of the programme.

    Smart Craft Community Node

    The programme will also explore a Smart Craft Community Node designed for remote craft and community environments. The system can support:

    • craft documentation
    • local data collection
    • field project monitoring
    • low-bandwidth mentor communication
